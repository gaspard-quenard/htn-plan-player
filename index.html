Files you can load

Files are recognised by their content: names and extensions do not matter. Load them together or one by one.

Plan (required)

The output of an HTN planner in the hierarchical plan format of the International Planning Competition (IPC 2020). The whole planner log is fine: the viewer reads the last block between ==> and <==.

==>
1 pick-up r1 p1 depot
2 move r1 depot a
root 10
10 deliver-all r1 -> m-deliver-next 11 12
11 deliver r1 p1 -> m-deliver 1 2
12 deliver-all r1 -> m-deliver-finished
<==
  • id action arguments: the actions, in execution order.
  • root ids: the initial task(s) of the problem.
  • id task arguments -> method subtask-ids: how each abstract task was decomposed. A method with no subtask ids (as m-deliver-finished above) is an empty decomposition.

Partial-order plans use the same format: their actions may interleave between subtasks.

Durations and parallel actions (optional)

End each action line with its start and end times, two integers with end > start:

==>
1 grind arabica 0 1
2 brew arabica cup1 1 4
3 slice bread1 1 2
4 toast bread1 2 3
root 10
…
  • Here brew lasts 3 time units, and slice then toast run during it.
  • Either every action has times, or none does. Without times, actions run one after the other, one time unit each.
  • An action's preconditions are checked when it starts; its effects are applied when it ends. When actions end and others start at the same time, the effects come first.

Domain and problem (optional)

HDDL files, as in the IPC hierarchical tracks. They add the definitions, the checks and the world state. Supported:

  • types, constants, objects, initial state, goal, initial task network;
  • tasks; methods with totally or partially ordered subtasks, preconditions and constraints;
  • actions with negative, disjunctive (or, imply), quantified (forall, exists) and equality preconditions, and conditional (when) and quantified effects.

Numeric fluents and durative actions in the domain are not supported yet.

Reading an HTN plan

A hierarchical plan is a tree. The root is the task the problem asks for. An abstract task (rounded box) is something to achieve, a method (small pill) is one way to break that task into smaller subtasks, and an action (box with a bar) is a step that is really executed.

Press play: the action executed now is blue, every task and method it belongs to is orange, finished parts are green, upcoming ones are grey. The bar above the player reads the path from the root to the current action: it explains why the action is executed.

In a partial-order plan, several branches can be in progress at the same time: their actions are interleaved.

Mouse

  • Click a box to see its definition, its preconditions (✓ / ✗ in the current state) and its effects.
  • Click an object name (a robot, a package, a location…) to highlight everything that involves it.
  • Double-click a box, or click its +N badge, to collapse / expand it.
  • Drag to move, wheel to zoom.
  • The timeline under the tree lists the actions in execution order: click one to jump to it.
